\textbf{Step 2 --- the VAE loss.}\; {\small A VAE trains on \textbf{two ideas at once}:
\opt{recon\_loss} (rebuild the picture) and \opt{kl\_loss} (keep the latent cloud tidy near the
origin). Both are already computed for you; your one line \textbf{combines} them, with the KL term
weighted by \opt{beta}.}

\textbf{Blank 1 --- \texttt{loss} (combine the two terms).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(a)] \opt{loss = recon\_loss + beta * kl\_loss}\; --- \textbf{add} the KL penalty (scaled by
    \opt{beta}) to the reconstruction. This is a VAE.
  \item[(b)] \opt{loss = recon\_loss - beta * kl\_loss}\; --- \emph{subtracting} the penalty
    \textbf{rewards} a messy latent; the space stops being sampleable. Wrong sign.
  \item[(c)] \opt{loss = recon\_loss * beta * kl\_loss}\; --- \emph{multiplying} is not the recipe;
    when \opt{kl\_loss} is near 0 the whole loss collapses to $\approx 0$. Wrong.
\end{itemize}
{\small (a) is the VAE objective (reconstruction $+\ \beta\cdot$KL). The self-check asserts
\opt{loss == recon\_loss + BETA*kl\_loss} (scalar, finite, KL $\geq 0$), so both the wrong sign (b)
and the wrong operator (c) \textbf{fail} the assert with a clear message.}

\vspace{3pt}\hrule\vspace{4pt}

\textbf{Step 3 --- KL by hand.}\; {\small No code --- a calculator. For one latent cloud with
$\text{mu}=[1,0]$, $\text{logvar}=[0,0]$, plug into
$\mathrm{KL}=-\tfrac12\sum_i\big(1+\text{logvar}_i-\text{mu}_i^2-e^{\text{logvar}_i}\big)$ with
$e^0=1$: dim~1 gives $1+0-1-1=-1$, dim~2 gives $1+0-0-1=0$, so the sum is $-1$ and
$\mathrm{KL}=-\tfrac12(-1)$.}

\textbf{Blank 2 --- \texttt{kl\_by\_hand} (one number).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(a)] \opt{kl\_by\_hand = 0.5}\; --- $-\tfrac12\times(-1)=0.5$. Matches the formula the code
    evaluates on the same numbers.
  \item[(b)] \opt{kl\_by\_hand = -0.5}\; --- this is the \emph{sum} times $\tfrac12$ \emph{without}
    the leading minus sign; the $-\tfrac12$ flips it to $+0.5$. Fails the check.
  \item[(c)] \opt{kl\_by\_hand = 1.0}\; --- dropped the factor $\tfrac12$. Fails the check.
\end{itemize}
{\small (a) is exact. The \texttt{assert} recomputes the same KL formula in code and demands your
number match to $10^{-6}$, so recompute carefully rather than guessing.}

\textbf{Step 8 --- the impostor threshold (+ a prediction).}\; {\small Reconstruction error is the
impostor score: clothes rebuild well (low), unseen digits rebuild badly (high). The rule: put the
line at the \textbf{90th percentile of the \emph{normal} (clothes) errors}, so about \textbf{10\%}
of real clothes are false alarms.}

\textbf{Blank 3a --- \texttt{THRESHOLD}.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(a)] \opt{THRESHOLD = np.percentile(normal\_errors, 90)}\; --- the 90th percentile of the
    \textbf{normal} errors, exactly the rule.
  \item[(b)] \opt{THRESHOLD = np.percentile(impostor\_errors, 90)}\; --- wrong array: you would
    calibrate on the \emph{digits} you are trying to catch, not the clothes.
  \item[(c)] \opt{THRESHOLD = normal\_errors.mean()}\; --- the mean sits far below the 90th
    percentile, so roughly half of all clothes would false-alarm.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Blank 3b --- \texttt{expected\_false\_alarms} (predict, then check).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(a)] \opt{expected\_false\_alarms = round(0.10 * len(normal\_errors))}\; --- by
    construction the 90th percentile leaves \textbf{10\%} of normals above the line.
  \item[(b)] \opt{expected\_false\_alarms = round(0.90 * len(normal\_errors))}\; --- confuses the
    percentile (90) with the false-alarm \emph{rate} (10\%).
  \item[(c)] \opt{expected\_false\_alarms = 0}\; --- a 90th-percentile cut always flags $\approx$10\%
    of normals, never 0.
\end{itemize}
{\small (a) for both. The prediction \texttt{assert} confirms the count you predicted is what the
threshold actually produces.}

\textbf{Step 9 --- draw your own impostor.}\; {\small Edit the 16$\times$16 ASCII grid: \opt{'\#'} is
ink, \opt{'.'} is blank. The rule is only about \textbf{format} --- keep \textbf{every row exactly 16
characters} and use only \opt{'\#'} and \opt{'.'}. The code upsamples it to 28$\times$28 and tells
you whether the VAE flags it as an impostor.}

\textbf{Blank 4 --- \texttt{DOODLE} (the drawing).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(a)] Any shape --- the shipped \textbf{spiral}, your initials, a squiggle, a molecule
    sketch --- as long as it is a list of \textbf{16 rows}, each exactly \textbf{16 chars} of only
    \opt{'\#'}/\opt{'.'}. This passes and gets scored against the threshold.
  \item[(b)] Rows of \emph{unequal} length, a wrong number of rows, or a stray character like
    \opt{'x'} or a space --- trips the grid sanity-check \opt{assert}.
  \item[(c)] An empty grid (all \opt{'.'}) --- runs, but there is nothing for the detector to judge;
    draw \emph{something} thin (lines and text flag easily; a big solid blob may slip through).
\end{itemize}
