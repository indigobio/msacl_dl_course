\vspace{4pt}\hrule\vspace{6pt}

\fillbox{1}{Problem}{What decision or measurement would the model make, and who uses the output?
E.g.\ ``flag likely-resistant isolates from the MALDI spectrum before culture comes back.''}

\fillbox{2}{Data \& representation}{Describe \emph{one} example exactly: shape, units, how many
samples, how they are labeled, and how imbalanced. E.g.\ ``one 6000-dim TIC-normalized spectrum;
$\sim$700 samples; 5--25\% positive; labeled by AST.''}

\fillbox{3}{Architecture}{Which model, and why does it fit \emph{this} representation? E.g.\ ``1D-CNN
--- peaks are local patterns anywhere along m/z, like Lab 2 / Track A''; or MLP for a tabular panel;
or transfer/re-head an existing model (Track C).}

\fillbox{4}{Validation plan}{What split (no leakage --- split by patient/batch, not by row!), which
metrics, and what target? E.g.\ ``stratified 80/20 by patient; sensitivity / specificity / AUROC +
confusion matrix; $\ge$0.90 sensitivity at a fixed specificity, threshold chosen on a validation set
--- never a single accuracy number.''}

\fillbox{5}{Why not a simpler model?}{What baseline must deep learning \emph{beat} to be worth it ---
logistic regression, a single-peak threshold, or always-guess-the-majority-class? If a one-line rule
already hits your target, deep learning may be the wrong tool --- and saying so is a valid, honest
conclusion (the Lecture 10 mindset).}
