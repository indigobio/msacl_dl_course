% =========================== Q1 ===========================
\textbf{1. Label each step: reason or act?}\; {\small An agent runs the \textbf{ReAct} loop
(\textcolor{teal}{\textbf{Thought}} $\rightarrow$ \textcolor{amber}{\textbf{Action}} $\rightarrow$
Observation $\rightarrow \dots \rightarrow$ Answer). A step is \textbf{R} (\emph{reason}) if it is a
\textbf{Thought} --- the agent planning or interpreting --- and \textbf{A} (\emph{act}) if it is an
\textbf{Action} --- the agent calling a tool (read, compute, write). The grey observation lines are the
tool's replies, not steps. Below is one transcript (an assistant asked to summarise this week's
peptide-ID counts and note any underperforming run). Write \textbf{R} or \textbf{A} beside each
numbered step.}

\vspace{5pt}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{@{}r@{\ \ }p{0.72\textwidth}c@{}}
\textbf{1.} & ``Before I can compare runs, I need to load \texttt{results.csv} and see its columns.''
            & \rb{R}{1.4cm} \\
\textbf{2.} & \texttt{read\_csv("results.csv")} & \rb{A}{1.4cm} \\
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\hspace{1.1em}\textcolor{muted}{\itshape $\rightarrow$ 6 runs $\cdot$ column: peptide\_ids}} & \\
\textbf{3.} & ``I'll compute the average ID count so I can see which runs fall below it.''
            & \rb{R}{1.4cm} \\
\textbf{4.} & compute the mean of \texttt{peptide\_ids} across the 6 runs & \rb{A}{1.4cm} \\
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\hspace{1.1em}\textcolor{muted}{\itshape $\rightarrow$ mean $= 1{,}850$ $\cdot$ run B-04 $= 420$}} & \\
\textbf{5.} & ``B-04 (420) is far below the mean of 1,850, so that run underperformed.''
            & \rb{R}{1.4cm} \\
\textbf{6.} & \texttt{write\_summary(flag="B-04")} & \rb{A}{1.4cm} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\keybox{\textbf{1 = R, 2 = A, 3 = R, 4 = A, 5 = R, 6 = A.}\; Odd steps are \emph{Thoughts} --- the agent
planning (1, 3) or interpreting a result (5) --- so they are \textbf{reason}. Even steps each call a tool
--- reading the file (2), computing the mean (4), writing the summary (6) --- so they are \textbf{act}.
The two grey lines are \emph{observations} (what a tool returned), not agent steps. The tell: a step that
only \emph{thinks/plans/interprets} is reason; a step that \emph{does something with a tool} is act.}

% =========================== Q2 ===========================
\textbf{2. Which guardrail for which failure?}\; {\small Match each failure mode to the ONE guardrail
that prevents it (each used once), using the word bank.}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{(a)} The agent writes a confident summary containing a \textbf{fabricated number or citation}.
\hfill $\rightarrow$\;\rb{Human review}{4.6cm}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{(b)} The agent \textbf{deletes or overwrites the raw data file} (or orders reagents) on its own.
\hfill $\rightarrow$\;\rb{Restricted tools}{4.6cm}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{(c)} A wrong result ships and \textbf{no one can tell what the agent did} or reproduce it.
\hfill $\rightarrow$\;\rb{Logging}{4.6cm}

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{center}
{\small \textbf{Word bank:}\;\; Human review \;$\cdot$\; Restricted tools \;$\cdot$\; Logging}
\end{center}

\keybox{(a) \textbf{Human review} --- a qualified person verifies every number and flag before anything
is released, so a fabricated value never reaches a report. (b) \textbf{Restricted tools} --- give the
agent read-only access and a limited toolset; no write, delete, or purchase without explicit approval, so
it \emph{cannot} take a destructive or irreversible action. (c) \textbf{Logging} --- record every
reason$\rightarrow$act step so the run is auditable and reproducible, so you can see exactly what happened
and why. Each guardrail is used once.}

% =========================== Q3 ===========================
\textbf{3. What must stay human-reviewed in a clinical workflow?}\; {\small An agent could help with each
step below. \textbf{Circle every step that a qualified human must review and sign before it reaches a
patient.}}

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\textbf{(a)} merge / reformat the QC tables &
\textbf{(b)} draft the QC summary text \\
\textbf{(c)} release the patient report / sign out results &
\textbf{(d)} the final resistance (R/S) call in the chart \\
\textbf{(e)} summarise 3 method papers & \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{3pt}
Circle all that must stay human-reviewed:\; \rb{(c) and (d)}{5.5cm}

\vspace{3pt}
In one sentence, what is the rule of thumb?

\work{1.0cm}
\ifsolution\else\blk{\linewidth}\fi

\keybox{\textbf{(c) and (d)} must stay human-reviewed and signed. Both become a \emph{released clinical
result} or a \emph{patient-affecting decision} --- releasing/signing a report (c) and the final
resistance (R/S) call (d) --- so a qualified person must verify and sign no matter how good the agent's
draft. Steps (a) merge/reformat tables, (b) draft the summary text, and (e) summarise papers are fine for
an agent to \emph{do} as a helper (still spot-checked, logged, and citations verified), because they do
not directly affect a patient without a human. \textbf{Rule of thumb:} anything that becomes a released
result or a patient-affecting decision needs the human gate --- the agent may draft, but a person reviews
and signs.}
