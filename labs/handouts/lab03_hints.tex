\textbf{Step 2 --- invent your symbol.}\; {\small Edit the ASCII grid so \texttt{'\#'} is ink and
\texttt{'.'} is blank. Every row must stay the \textbf{same length}, and you want a \textbf{bold,
centered} shape (roughly 10--40\% ink) --- thin one-pixel lines are too faint to learn.}

\textbf{Blank 1 --- \texttt{SYMBOL} (the drawing).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(a)] A bold, centered shape --- e.g. a heart with two round lobes on top and a point at the
    bottom, its widest rows a solid run of about a dozen \opt{'\#'}. Any heart / star / letter /
    initial drawn this boldly passes.
  \item[(b)] A single thin line (one \opt{'\#'} per row) --- too little ink (fails the
    \opt{0.03 < ink < 0.6} check, and too faint to learn).
  \item[(c)] Every cell set to \opt{'\#'} (the whole grid filled) --- too much ink (fails the same
    check; a solid block has no shape to recognize).
\end{itemize}
{\small (a) is the goal. (b) and (c) trip the ink-amount \texttt{assert}; rows of unequal length
also error when the grid is parsed.}

\vspace{3pt}\hrule\vspace{4pt}

\textbf{Step 3 --- the diffusion training objective.}\; {\small The model was handed a noisy image
and guessed the noise (\texttt{noise\_pred}); the truth is \texttt{noise}. Score how far the guess
is from the truth with \textbf{mean-squared error} and store it in \texttt{loss}.}

\textbf{Blank 2 --- the loss line.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(a)] \opt{loss = F.mse\_loss(noise\_pred, noise)}
  \item[(b)] \opt{loss = F.mse\_loss(noise\_pred, noisy)}
  \item[(c)] \opt{loss = F.cross\_entropy(noise\_pred, noise)}
\end{itemize}
{\small (a) is right --- diffusion training makes the \emph{predicted noise} match the \emph{real
noise} you added. (b) compares the guess to the noisy \emph{image}, not the noise --- wrong target.
(c) cross-entropy is for class labels, not for matching two continuous noise images (it errors here).}

\vspace{3pt}\hrule\vspace{4pt}

\textbf{Step 4 --- the fine-tune learning rate.}\; {\small You are \emph{nudging} a model that
already knows how to draw, not training from scratch --- so use a rate \textbf{much smaller} than a
from-scratch rate. The check is \opt{0 < FINETUNE\_LR < 1e-2}.}

\textbf{Blank 3 --- \texttt{FINETUNE\_LR}.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(a)] \opt{FINETUNE\_LR = 1e-4}
  \item[(b)] \opt{FINETUNE\_LR = 1e-1}
  \item[(c)] \opt{FINETUNE\_LR = 1e-6}
\end{itemize}
{\small (a) is the sweet spot --- big enough to pick up your symbol in $\sim$400 steps, small enough
not to wreck the pretrained model. (b) is far too big: it blows the model apart and the AFTER grid is
garbage (and it exceeds the \opt{< 1e-2} check). (c) is so tiny the model barely moves --- the AFTER
samples still look like digits and the similarity \texttt{assert} fails.}
